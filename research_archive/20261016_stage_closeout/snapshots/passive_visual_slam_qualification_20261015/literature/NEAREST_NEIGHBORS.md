## DynaPix source details

`motion_tum.py`: `get_movable()` combines clipped and normalized background differences; `search_pose()` reads the pose table; `generate_masks()` warps previous and future frames, invokes FlowFormer differences, then writes `(1-p_motion)*1000`. Thus its saved `prob` values represent static confidence, not the opposite convention. `Tracking.cc` copies confidence into keyframes and checks it during map-point admission. `Optimizer.cc` reads `mImProb`, fixes per-feature weight vertices, retains Huber optimization and marks low-static-confidence map points bad. The base variant is ORB-SLAM2; DynaPix_D uses DynaSLAM.

The arithmetic/background-difference and evaluation modules are separable reading references. The complete motion probability pipeline is not a dependency-free drop-in module: it needs background images, warping transforms and optical flow. Public result links are useful for future verification, but an ORB-SLAM2 result beside ORB-SLAM3 is not a same-backend ablation.

## Decision boundary

No new algorithm is claimed by this audit. This round must first establish a usable native baseline and actual error/loss intervals. Dynamic probability weighting, ordinary robust kernels, temporal dynamic identification, feature-count adaptation and generic geometry quality are already crowded mechanisms. A specific residual beyond simple masks remains UNKNOWN until a controlled comparison.
